04 Where To Spend Your Time

Prioritize

  • Cell Injury & Cellular Adaptation and Specific Specimen Examination are tied at 20 marks each — the two pillars of this subject.
  • Memorize the cell-injury/adaptation terminology table (necrosis, apoptosis, atrophy, hypertrophy, hyperplasia, metaplasia, dysplasia) cold — it's pure Group A gold.
  • Learn the named-disease essay format (agent → transmission → pathogenesis → diagnosis → treatment) once for Unit 4's 8 diseases.

Deprioritize

  • Don't skip Neoplasia's terminology (benign/malignant/metastasis) thinking it's minor — differentiate-between questions on this repeat often.

06 Cell Injury and Cellular Adaptation (20 marks · 35 hrs)

The highest-marks unit in this subject — a quarter of the total. Definitions of core terminology are the single best exam investment here.

TermMeaning
NecrosisPathological cell death from injury
ApoptosisProgrammed (controlled) cell death
Hypoxia / IschemiaReduced oxygen supply / reduced blood supply to tissue
AtrophyDecrease in cell/organ size
HypertrophyIncrease in cell size
HyperplasiaIncrease in cell number
MetaplasiaReversible change of one differentiated cell type to another
DysplasiaAbnormal, disordered cell growth (pre-cancerous)
AplasiaFailure of an organ/tissue to develop

Mechanism of cell injury (biochemical basis)

  • Decreased ATP production
  • Mitochondrial damage
  • Calcium influx and homeostasis disruption
  • Accumulation of oxygen-derived free radicals

Know the difference between reversible and irreversible cell injury, the types of necrosis, and the forms of cellular adaptation (atrophy, hypertrophy, hyperplasia, metaplasia). Also: transplant rejection (introduction/classification) and autoimmune diseases (definition, classification, pathogenesis, lab findings).

07 Inflammation (9 marks · 15 hrs)

Key terminology: oedema, erythema, vasodilation, granuloma, acute, chronic, abscess, exudates, and transudates.

Types, causes, and mechanism of acute inflammation vs chronic inflammation, and the chemical mediators of inflammation.

08 Infection (17 marks · 30 hrs)

Classification and mechanism of infection. Key terminology: agent, host, vector, symptoms, signs, diagnosis, prognosis, therapy, prophylaxis, incubation period, window period.

For each disease below, know: etiological agent, route of transmission, pathogenesis, clinical manifestation, laboratory diagnosis, and treatment — in the Nepalese context specifically.

  • Candidiasis
  • Leprosy
  • Enteric fever
  • Cholera
  • Scrub typhus
  • HIV/AIDS
  • Infectious hepatitis
  • Japanese encephalitis

09 Neoplasia (9 marks · 15 hrs)

Key terminology: benign, malignant, metastasis, carcinoma, lymphoma, sarcoma, papilloma, adenoma, anaplasia, pleomorphism.

A classic exam question: differentiate between benign and malignant tumors. Also know: etiology of cancer (carcinogenic agents), tumor markers (definition, classification, applications), and laboratory diagnosis of cancer.

10 Specific Specimen Examination for Laboratory Diagnosis (20 marks · 35 hrs)

Tied with Unit 2 for the highest marks in the subject. For each fluid type, know indications, specimen collection, and physical/chemical/microscopic/microbiological investigation.

  • Examination of effusions (synovial, pleural, pericardial, peritoneal fluids)
  • Examination of urine — composition of normal urine, specimen collection, investigations
  • Examination of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) — composition, collection, changes during disease

Semen examination

  • Specimen collection
  • Volume, appearance, liquefaction time
  • Fructose estimation and pH measurement
  • Percentage of motile/viable spermatozoa
  • Sperm count
  • Percentage of spermatozoa with normal morphology

11 Practice Questions — Clinical Pathology

Group A · 1 mark each
  1. Define necrosis.
  2. What is the difference between hypertrophy and hyperplasia?
  3. Define metastasis.
  4. Name two chemical mediators of inflammation.
  5. Define incubation period.
Group B · 3–5 marks
  1. Differentiate between benign and malignant tumors.
  2. Differentiate between leukemoid reaction... (see Hematology) — explain acute vs chronic inflammation instead, with causes and mechanism.
  3. Explain the mechanism of cell injury on a biochemical basis.
Group C · 8–10 marks
  1. Describe the etiological agent, transmission, pathogenesis, clinical features, and laboratory diagnosis of HIV/AIDS in the Nepalese context.
  2. Discuss the types of cell injury and the forms of cellular adaptation, with examples.
  3. Describe the physical, chemical, and microscopic examination of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and its diagnostic significance.
