04 Where To Spend Your Time

Prioritize

  • Microtomy, Staining Technique, and Cytopathology are tied at 11 marks each — the three biggest units.
  • Learn the tissue-processing sequence (fixation → dehydration → clearing → infiltration → embedding) as an ordered list — it's asked directly, in order.
  • H&E staining is the single most important named procedure in this subject — know its full principle and procedure.

Deprioritize

  • Don't skip Mounting Media (4 hrs) just because it's short — at 5 marks it's still worth more than its time investment suggests.

05 Histopathology — Introduction (7 marks · 8 hrs)

General concept of histology vs histopathology. Key terminology: autopsy, excisional biopsy, incisional biopsy.

  • Role of histopathology in disease diagnosis
  • Basic requirements and organization of a histopathology laboratory
  • Receiving and labelling specimens
  • Grossing technique and specimen management
  • Hazards of biological specimens and their disposal

06 Fixation (5 marks · 6 hrs)

Fixation preserves tissue structure and prevents decay. Know the properties, classification, and modes of action of fixatives, and their indications.

Preparation of common fixatives: 10% formalin, 10% formal-saline, Neutral Buffered Formalin, Zenker's fluid, Bouin's fluid, Clark's fluid, Carnoy's fluid.

07 Tissue Processing (9 marks · 10 hrs)

Factors affecting fixation, then the processing sequence: dehydration (common dehydrating agents) → clearing (common clearing agents) → infiltration and impregnation → embedding.

  • Different types of embedding media, with advantages/disadvantages
  • Automated Tissue Processor — types, working principle, components, procedure, operation and maintenance

08 Decalcification (9 marks · 10 hrs)

Introduction and importance of decalcification (removing calcium from bone/calcified tissue before sectioning). Examples and composition of common decalcifying agents, methods, factors affecting decalcification, assessment of completeness, and neutralization of acid.

09 Microtomy (11 marks · 12 hrs)

The single highest-marks unit in this subject. Use of the microtome; types (with advantages/disadvantages); tissue-section cutting using a rotary microtome; care and maintenance.

Microtome knives — selection, honing and stropping, automatic knife sharpener, care and maintenance.

10 Frozen Sections / Cryostat (5 marks · 6 hrs)

Define cryostat and its clinical importance (rapid intra-operative diagnosis). Technique of frozen sectioning using a cryostat, and rapid staining of frozen sections.

11 Staining Technique (11 marks · 12 hrs)

Terminology: mordant, dye, blueing, differentiation, accelerator. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) — the core stain: principle, reagent preparation, procedure, observation.

Principle, requirements, procedure & results of:

  • PAS (Periodic Acid-Schiff)
  • Alcian blue
  • Ziehl-Neelsen
  • Congo red
  • Sudan black
  • Perl's stain
  • MPO stain

12 Mounting Media (5 marks · 4 hrs)

Introduction and importance of mounting media; types of mounting media; the mounting procedure for a finished slide.

13 Basic Concept of Immunohistochemistry (7 marks · 8 hrs)

Introduction and principle of immunohistochemistry (IHC); steps/procedure; clinical significance; examples of common IHC markers used diagnostically.

14 Cytopathology (11 marks · 12 hrs)

Introduction, scope, and importance of cytopathology. Exfoliative cytology — specimen collection (gynecological and non-gynecological), cytosmear preparation from viscid secretions, CSF and other body fluids, sputum, urine, bronchial aspirate.

Fixation of cytology specimens — fixatives, advantages/disadvantages.

Principle, requirements, procedure & results of:

  • Papanicolaou (PAP) stain
  • Giemsa stain
  • May Grünwald-Giemsa (MGG) stain

Also: Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC) — importance, requirements, procedure, advantages/disadvantages; sampling, staining, and demonstration of Barr body.

15 Practice Questions — Histopathology and Cytopathology

Group A · 1 mark each
  1. Define fixation.
  2. Name two common decalcifying agents.
  3. What does H&E stand for?
  4. Define cryostat.
  5. Name the stain used to demonstrate Barr body.
Group B · 3–5 marks
  1. Explain the steps of tissue processing from fixation to embedding.
  2. Describe the principle and procedure of the PAS stain.
  3. Differentiate between exfoliative cytology and FNAC.
Group C · 8–10 marks
  1. Describe the principle, reagent preparation, procedure, and clinical significance of Hematoxylin & Eosin (H&E) staining.
  2. Explain the types of microtomes and the procedure for cutting tissue sections with a rotary microtome.
  3. Discuss the collection, fixation, and staining procedure for a Papanicolaou (PAP) smear.
