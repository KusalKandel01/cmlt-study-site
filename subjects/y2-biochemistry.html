04 Where To Spend Your Time

Prioritize

  • Biomolecules (23 marks) and Metabolism (20 marks) together are more than half the subject — start there.
  • Diabetes Mellitus and the urea cycle are named, high-yield Group C essay topics — memorize them as structured answers.
  • Reuse your Year 1 Chemistry knowledge of moles, solutions, and buffers directly in Unit 2 — it's the same content, re-applied.

Deprioritize

  • Don't treat Introduction to Biochemistry (1 mark) as anything more than a 2-minute skim.
  • Don't memorize every analyte's full procedure in Unit 6 — focus on principle and clinical significance, which is what Group B/C actually asks.

05 Introduction to Biochemistry (1 marks · 2 hrs)

Definition of biochemistry; its scope, fields, and importance; a brief history of the subject — a quick, easy Group A unit.

06 Elementary Chemistry (7 marks · 12 hrs)

Core definitions: atom, molecule, atomic number, atomic weight, molecular weight, equivalent weight, specific gravity, and chemical equations (oxidation/reduction) — direct carryover from Year 1 Chemistry.

Solutions

  • Types of solutions — saturated, unsaturated, supersaturated, normal, molar, molal, and percentage solutions
  • Preparation of normal, molar, and percentage solutions at different concentrations
  • Buffer and standard solutions
  • Osmosis and diffusion

07 Physical Chemistry — Lab Instruments (9 marks · 15 hrs)

Principle, function, handling, and maintenance of core biochemistry lab instruments:

  • Water bath
  • Centrifuge
  • Analytical balances
  • Colorimeters
  • Spectrophotometers
  • Micro-pipettes

Also know: glassware composition/use and cleaning, changes occurring in blood after collection, and laboratory hazards/precautions specific to the clinical chemistry lab.

08 Biomolecules (23 marks · 40 hrs)

The single biggest unit in the whole subject — nearly 30% of the marks. Introduction, types, and importance of biomolecules.

Carbohydrates

  • Introduction, definition, functions
  • Classification in detail — monosaccharides, disaccharides (sucrose, maltose, lactose), oligosaccharides, polysaccharides (homo- and hetero-)

Proteins

  • Introduction, definition, and functions of proteins
  • Amino acids — introduction, properties, classification by structure/nutritional requirement/metabolic fate
  • Classification of proteins — functional, chemical/solubility, nutritional
  • Structure, denaturation (definition, agents, characteristics), coagulation and flocculation

Lipids

  • Introduction, definition, functions, classification
  • Triglycerides — hydrolysis, saponification, rancidity, lipid peroxidation
  • Fatty acids — saturated vs unsaturated; essential fatty acids and deficiency
  • Phospholipids and cholesterol

Plasma proteins, enzymes & vitamins

  • Plasma proteins — types, functions and normal range, abnormalities, electrophoretic pattern
  • Enzymes
    • Definition and classification
    • Factors affecting enzyme activity
    • Active site features; enzyme inhibition; mechanism of action
    • Coenzymes and isoenzymes (LDH, CPK, ALP)
    • Diagnostic importance of enzymes in disease
  • Vitamins
    • Definition and classification (fat-soluble vs water-soluble)
    • Dietary sources, RDA, physiological roles
    • Deficiency manifestations of each vitamin
  • Minerals — biochemical functions and deficiency effects of Iron, Calcium, Phosphorus, Sodium, Potassium, and Iodine
  • Hormones — introduction, definition, functions, classification
  • Tumor markers — introduction, classification, significance
  • Basic concept of nucleic acids — nucleoside, nucleotide, nucleic acid; DNA vs RNA

09 Metabolism of Biomolecules (20 marks · 35 hrs)

Carbohydrate metabolism

  • Major pathways and entry of glucose into cells
  • Glycolysis — salient features, pathway, energetics, regulation, clinical significance
  • Gluconeogenesis — importance and pathway
  • Brief: Cori's cycle, Rapoport-Luebering cycle, HMP shunt pathway, glycogen metabolism
  • Regulation of blood glucose level, including hormonal regulation
  • Diabetes Mellitus — high-yield essay topic
    • Introduction and types
    • Cardinal symptoms
    • Complications
    • Laboratory diagnosis and management
  • Glucose Tolerance Test — patient prep, procedure, interpretation

Protein metabolism

  • Amino acid pool; overview of amino acid metabolism
  • Transamination and deamination (oxidative/non-oxidative)
  • Urea cycle — pathway, energetics, regulation, clinical significance of blood urea (BUN)
  • Non-protein nitrogenous substances — creatine, creatinine, uric acid, bilirubin

Lipid metabolism

  • Fatty acid oxidation pathways
  • β-oxidation of fatty acids — pathway, energetics, regulation, clinical significance
  • Ketone bodies — ketogenesis, ketolysis, clinical significance
  • Fatty liver and associated health risks

10 Estimation of Biochemical Analytes (17 marks · 30 hrs)

For each analyte below, know the method, principle, procedure, reference range, clinical significance, and precautions:

  • Sugar
  • Urea
  • Creatinine
  • Uric acid
  • Serum bilirubin (total & direct)
  • Total protein
  • Albumin, A/G ratio
  • SGPT/ALT, SGOT/AST
  • Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)
  • Lipid profile — triglycerides, total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, VLDL
  • Amylase
  • Serum calcium and phosphorous
  • Serum electrolytes (Na, K)

Organ function tests — introduction & interpretation

  • Liver Function Tests (LFT)
  • Renal Function Tests (RFT)
  • Pancreatic Function Tests (PFT)
  • Cardiac Function Tests (CFT)
  • Thyroid Function Tests (TFT)

11 Quality Control in Clinical Chemistry Laboratory (3 marks · 6 hrs)

Effects of time, pH, temperature, and light on chemical reagents; preservation of chemicals used in the biochemistry lab; general Quality Control (QC) principles.

12 Practice Questions — Clinical Biochemistry

Group A · 1 mark each
  1. Define biomolecule.
  2. Name the two types of amino acid deamination.
  3. What is the normal function of ALP?
  4. Define ketogenesis.
  5. Name any two fat-soluble vitamins.
Group B · 3–5 marks
  1. Explain the classification of enzymes and the factors affecting enzyme activity.
  2. Describe the pathway and clinical significance of glycolysis.
  3. Differentiate between isoenzymes and coenzymes with examples.
Group C · 8–10 marks
  1. Describe the types, cardinal symptoms, complications, laboratory diagnosis, and management of Diabetes Mellitus.
  2. Explain the classification, functions, and deficiency manifestations of vitamins.
  3. Describe the urea cycle and its clinical significance in relation to blood urea nitrogen (BUN).
