1 Unit 3: Body Fluid & Electrolyte (10 marks — the biggest unit)

This is the unit most students skip because it doesn't map to a body "organ" — and it's worth more than any single organ system.

Fluid compartments

  • Intracellular fluid (ICF) — fluid inside cells
  • Extracellular fluid (ECF) — fluid outside cells (plasma + interstitial fluid)

Acid-base balance — 3 mechanisms (Group B favorite)

  1. Respiratory system — regulates CO₂ (and therefore carbonic acid) levels via breathing rate
  2. Urinary system — excretes H⁺ ions and reabsorbs bicarbonate
  3. Buffer mechanism — chemical systems (e.g., bicarbonate buffer) that resist pH change instantly

Electrolyte imbalances — high-yield definitions

TermMeaning
Hyponatremia / HypernatremiaLow / high blood sodium
Hypokalemia / HyperkalemiaLow / high blood potassium
DehydrationFluid volume deficit
Isotonic / Hypotonic / HypertonicSolution concentration relative to body fluid — determines direction of water movement, directly relevant to IV fluid selection

3 Cell, Tissue & Blood

Cell partFunction
Cell membraneControls what enters/leaves the cell
NucleusControls the cell, contains DNA
MitochondriaProduces energy (ATP)

4 tissue types: Epithelial (covers/lines), Connective (bone, blood, cartilage), Muscular (movement), Nervous (impulses). Cell division: Mitosis (growth/repair, identical daughter cells) vs Meiosis (gamete production, half chromosome number).

Blood composition: Plasma (liquid, carries nutrients), RBC (oxygen via hemoglobin), WBC (infection defense), Platelets (clotting). Blood grouping and Rhesus factor are explicitly named in the curriculum — expect a direct question.

4 Circulatory System — Heart Diagram (Group C)

  1. Opening definition: "The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body."
  2. 4 chambers: 2 atria (upper) + 2 ventricles (lower)
  3. Cardiac cycle and conduction system — named explicitly in the curriculum, likely tested
  4. 4 circulation types: Pulmonary, Systemic, Portal, Fetal — list all four, a common Group A trap is forgetting Portal/Fetal
  5. Diagram: label 4 chambers + aorta, pulmonary artery, vena cava, pulmonary veins

5 Skeletal, Respiratory & Digestive — Quick Reference

  • Skeletal (6 marks): Bone types (long/short/flat/irregular/sesamoid); Axial vs Appendicular skeleton; Joint types (fixed/cartilaginous/synovial); named disorders — Osteoporosis, Rickets, Arthritis
  • Respiratory (6 marks): Nose → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Alveoli; External vs Internal respiration; lung volumes (tidal volume, vital capacity) are named — expect definitions
  • Digestive (2 marks, lower-yield): Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine; accessory organs: salivary glands, pancreas, liver

6 Nervous, Reproductive & Endocrine — Quick Reference

  • Nervous (5 marks): CNS (brain+spinal cord) vs PNS (spinal+cranial nerves) vs ANS (sympathetic/parasympathetic); Brain parts: cerebrum, cerebellum, brain stem, mid brain
  • Reproductive (6 marks): Puberty, menstruation cycle, spermatogenesis/oogenesis, fertilization — named processes, expect direct definition questions
  • Endocrine (6 marks): Glands and their hormones; sex hormones specifically named: Estrogen, Progesterone, Testosterone

7 Sensory Organs & Growth (lower marks, still testable)

Skin: sensation + temperature regulation (conduction, convection, radiation, excretion). Eye: structure, accommodation, visual pathway. Ear: structure and function. Growth stages: Neonatal → Infancy → Childhood → Adolescence → Adulthood, plus fetal circulation changes at birth.
