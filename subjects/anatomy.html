✓ What To Do / What To Skip

DO

  • Memorize the 10-system table cold — it answers most Group A and B questions across the whole subject.
  • Actually study Unit 3 (Body Fluid & Electrolyte) even though it doesn't feel like "real anatomy" — it's worth more than any organ system.
  • Practice drawing the heart diagram (4 chambers + 4 vessels) until it takes under 2 minutes.
  • Learn definitions in exact-phrase form ("Cell = the basic structural and functional unit of life") — Group A rewards precise recall.

DON'T

  • Don't give equal study time to all 15 units — Digestive (2 marks) and Special Sensory (2 marks) need far less time than Body Fluid & Electrolyte (10) or Circulatory (6).
  • Don't skip Unit 3 because it's unfamiliar-sounding — that's exactly why other students skip it too, which makes it a differentiator.
  • Don't try to deeply understand every mechanism — for Group A/B, a memorized definition beats a half-understood explanation.

1 Unit 3: Body Fluid & Electrolyte (10 marks — the biggest unit)

This is the unit most students skip because it doesn't map to a body "organ" — and it's worth more than any single organ system.

Fluid compartments

  • Intracellular fluid (ICF) — fluid inside cells
  • Extracellular fluid (ECF) — fluid outside cells (plasma + interstitial fluid)

Acid-base balance — 3 mechanisms (Group B favorite)

  1. Respiratory system — regulates CO₂ (and therefore carbonic acid) levels via breathing rate
  2. Urinary system — excretes H⁺ ions and reabsorbs bicarbonate
  3. Buffer mechanism — chemical systems (e.g., bicarbonate buffer) that resist pH change instantly

Electrolyte imbalances — high-yield definitions

TermMeaning
Hyponatremia / HypernatremiaLow / high blood sodium
Hypokalemia / HyperkalemiaLow / high blood potassium
DehydrationFluid volume deficit
Isotonic / Hypotonic / HypertonicSolution concentration relative to body fluid — determines direction of water movement, directly relevant to IV fluid selection

3 Cell, Tissue & Blood

Cell partFunction
Cell membraneControls what enters/leaves the cell
NucleusControls the cell, contains DNA
MitochondriaProduces energy (ATP)

4 tissue types: Epithelial (covers/lines), Connective (bone, blood, cartilage), Muscular (movement), Nervous (impulses). Cell division: Mitosis (growth/repair, identical daughter cells) vs Meiosis (gamete production, half chromosome number).

Blood composition: Plasma (liquid, carries nutrients), RBC (oxygen via hemoglobin), WBC (infection defense), Platelets (clotting). Blood grouping and Rhesus factor are explicitly named in the curriculum — expect a direct question.

4 Circulatory System — Heart Diagram (Group C)

  1. Opening definition: "The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body."
  2. 4 chambers: 2 atria (upper) + 2 ventricles (lower)
  3. Cardiac cycle and conduction system — named explicitly in the curriculum, likely tested
  4. 4 circulation types: Pulmonary, Systemic, Portal, Fetal — list all four, a common Group A trap is forgetting Portal/Fetal
  5. Diagram: label 4 chambers + aorta, pulmonary artery, vena cava, pulmonary veins

5 Unit 7: Skeletal System (6 marks)

Bone — a hard connective tissue forming the skeleton. Joint — the point where two bones meet.

Bone development & structure

Bones develop through ossification. Sutures are fixed joints between skull bones; fontanelles ("soft spots") are the unossified gaps in an infant's skull that allow growth and passage through the birth canal.

Bone types (by shape)

TypeExample
LongFemur
ShortCarpals (wrist)
FlatSkull bones, ribs
IrregularVertebrae
SesamoidPatella (kneecap)

Classification of the skeleton

  • Axial skeleton — skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum (the body's central axis)
  • Appendicular skeleton — limbs, shoulder girdle, pelvic girdle

Joint types

TypeMovementExample
Fixed (fibrous)NoneSkull sutures
CartilaginousSlightBetween vertebrae
SynovialFreeKnee, elbow, shoulder

Joint movement works on the principle of a lever — bones act as levers, joints as fulcrums, muscles provide the force.

Named disorders (introduction level — Group A)

  • Osteoporosis — bones become weak and brittle due to loss of bone density
  • Rickets — softening of bones in children, usually from Vitamin D deficiency
  • Osteomalacia — adult equivalent of rickets
  • Osteomyelitis — bone infection
  • Arthritis — joint inflammation
  • Fracture — a break in the bone

6 Unit 8: Respiratory System (6 marks)

Pathway of air

Nose/nasal cavity → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli (where gas exchange happens). Respiratory muscles: the diaphragm and intercostal muscles drive breathing.

External vs Internal respiration

  • External respiration — gas exchange between lungs (alveoli) and blood
  • Internal respiration — gas exchange between blood and body tissues

Respiration relates to Boyle's Law — as lung volume increases (inhalation), pressure decreases, drawing air in; the reverse happens on exhalation.

Lung capacity terms (Group A gold — these are named and defined exactly)

TermMeaning
Tidal volumeAir moved in one normal breath
Inspiratory reserve volumeExtra air that can be inhaled beyond a normal breath
Expiratory reserve volumeExtra air that can be exhaled beyond a normal breath
Residual volumeAir remaining in lungs after maximum exhalation
Vital capacityMaximum air that can be exhaled after maximum inhalation

7 Unit 9: Digestive System (2 marks — lower yield, still testable)

Digestion — the breakdown of food into absorbable nutrients.

Organs of the alimentary canal

Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus.

Accessory organs

  • Salivary glands (pairs) — begin carbohydrate digestion via saliva
  • Liver — produces bile
  • Pancreas — produces digestive enzymes and insulin
  • Biliary duct — carries bile from liver/gallbladder to small intestine

Teeth — temporary (milk) teeth in childhood, replaced by permanent teeth in adulthood.

8 Unit 10: Urinary System (5 marks)

Organs

Kidney → Ureter → Urinary bladder → Urethra.

Urine formation — 3-step mechanism (Group B favorite)

  1. Glomerular filtration — blood is filtered under pressure in the glomerulus
  2. Selective reabsorption — useful substances (glucose, water, salts) are reabsorbed back into blood
  3. Tubular secretion — waste products are actively added into the forming urine

Micturition — the medical term for urination; the mechanism by which the bladder empties.

9 Unit 11: Nervous System (5 marks)

Basic components

Neuron — the nerve cell. Neuroglia — supporting cells of the nervous system. Synapse — the junction where one neuron transmits a signal to the next.

Three divisions

DivisionMade of
Central Nervous System (CNS)Brain + Spinal cord
Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)Spinal nerves + Cranial nerves
Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)Sympathetic + Parasympathetic systems

Brain parts

  • Cerebrum — largest part; thought, voluntary movement, senses
  • Cerebellum — balance and coordination
  • Mid brain — relays sensory/motor signals
  • Brain stem — controls involuntary functions (breathing, heart rate)
  • Meninges — protective covering membranes of the brain/spinal cord

Reflex action — an automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus, bypassing conscious brain processing for speed (e.g., pulling your hand from something hot).

10 Unit 12: Reproductive System (6 marks)

Female

External and internal reproductive organs; primary and secondary sex characteristics. Puberty — the stage of sexual maturation. Menstrual cycle — the monthly hormonal cycle preparing the body for possible pregnancy.

Fertilization terms (Group A gold)

  • Spermatogenesis — sperm production (male)
  • Oogenesis — egg production (female)
  • Conception — fertilization of egg by sperm
  • Menopause — permanent end of menstrual cycles

Male

Structure and function of male reproductive organs; primary/secondary sex characteristics; puberty.

11 Unit 13: Endocrine System (6 marks)

Endocrine glands secrete hormones directly into the bloodstream (no ducts, unlike exocrine glands).

12 Unit 14: Special Sensory Organs (2 marks)

Skin

Functions: sensation, temperature regulation (via conduction, convection, radiation, excretion/sweating), protection, and immunity.

Eye

Structure and function; accommodation — the eye's ability to adjust focus for near/far objects; visual pathway — the route visual signals travel from retina to brain.

Ear

Structure and function, both adult and child (children's ear anatomy differs slightly, relevant clinically). Nose/throat and tongue are reviewed from the respiratory and digestive units respectively.
