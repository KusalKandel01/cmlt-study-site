Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism — smallest to largest. This alone answers several Group A marks on its own.

3 Key Definitions (Group A)

  • Cell — the basic structural and functional unit of life (the single most-asked definition in the subject — memorize this exact phrasing)
  • Homeostasis — the body's ability to maintain a stable internal environment
  • Metabolism — all chemical reactions in the body that release or use energy
  • Tissue — a group of similar cells performing the same function

5 The 4 Tissue Types

Memorize this list cold — asked almost every year in some form.

  1. Epithelial — covers and lines body surfaces and organs
  2. Connective — connects and supports other tissues (bone, blood, cartilage)
  3. Muscular — enables movement through contraction
  4. Nervous — transmits nerve impulses

6 Blood

Definition: a fluid connective tissue that circulates through the body carrying oxygen, nutrients, and waste.

ComponentFunction
PlasmaLiquid part — carries nutrients, hormones, waste
RBC (erythrocytes)Carries oxygen (via hemoglobin)
WBC (leukocytes)Fights infection
Platelets (thrombocytes)Helps blood clot

Group B model answer — "Write the functions of blood"

This point-list pattern works for almost any "write the functions of X" question — 5 numbered points = full 5-mark answer.

  1. Transports oxygen and nutrients to body cells
  2. Removes carbon dioxide and waste products
  3. Helps regulate body temperature
  4. Fights infection through white blood cells
  5. Helps blood clot to prevent excessive bleeding

7 The Nervous System, Briefly

Neuron — the basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system. Three main parts: dendrites (receive signals), cell body (contains nucleus), axon (sends signals onward).

The brain has three main parts:

  • Cerebrum — largest part; controls thought, voluntary movement, and senses
  • Cerebellum — controls balance and coordination
  • Brainstem — controls involuntary functions (breathing, heart rate)

8 High-Frequency Values (fill-in-the-blank)

  • Normal body temperature: 37°C (98.6°F)
  • Normal pulse rate (adult): 72 beats/minute
  • Normal blood pressure: 120/80 mmHg
  • Normal respiratory rate (adult): 12–20 breaths/minute

9 Group C Model — Heart Diagram

"Describe the structure of the heart with a labeled diagram" — here's how to structure a full 8–10 mark answer:

  1. Opening definition: "The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body."
  2. 4 chambers: 2 atria (upper) + 2 ventricles (lower)
  3. Major vessels: aorta, pulmonary artery, vena cava, pulmonary veins
  4. Diagram: label the 4 chambers + 4 vessels — worth marks on its own, even before you write anything
  5. Closing line: "The heart pumps oxygenated blood to the body and deoxygenated blood to the lungs."

A rough, labeled diagram earns marks even if your written explanation is thin. Practice drawing this from memory until it takes under 2 minutes.
