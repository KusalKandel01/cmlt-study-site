✓ What To Do / What To Skip

DO

  • Total Quality Management (15 marks) is the single biggest unit — memorize the QA terminology (accuracy, precision, SD, CV, sensitivity, specificity) cold.
  • Lab Hazards/Safety and Inventory Management are tied at 9 marks each — both are concrete, list-based, and fast to memorize.
  • Bio-safety and bio-security (BSL 1–4, risk groups) is a clean, self-contained topic — high marks-per-minute-studied.

DON'T

  • Don't neglect Medical Ethics thinking it's soft/non-technical — the accrediting-body list (NPHL, ISO, NABL, CAP, NHPC) is pure Group A recall.

Ch. 1 Introduction to Clinical Laboratory Management (9 marks · 10 hrs)

  • Introduction and objective of laboratory management
  • Laboratory management framework
  • National healthcare delivery system of Nepal, regarding the laboratory profession
  • Code of conduct of the medical laboratory profession
  • Level of clinical laboratories according to complexity and national guidelines

Ch. 2 Departments/Sections of a Clinical Laboratory (5 marks · 6 hrs)

Different sections of a clinical laboratory, their services, and their uses:

  • Patient communication (report delivery)
  • Sample collection
  • Haematology
  • Blood banking
  • Clinical biochemistry
  • Microbiology
  • Serology/immunology
  • Parasitology
  • Histopathology & cytopathology
  • Emergency lab
  • Molecular laboratory

Ch. 3 Laboratory Design (5 marks · 6 hrs)

Basic concept of laboratory organization:

  • Location and space requirement
  • Light, ventilation, temperature control, humidity
  • Electrical and water supply
  • Sanitation and noise control
  • Use of fume hood/safety hood
  • Human resource at different levels of the laboratory

Ch. 4 Laboratory Work Flow (5 marks · 6 hrs)

The description of laboratory work flow: appropriate specimen collection → specimen organization and transport → laboratory investigation → report preparation and patient communication.

Ch. 5 Personnel Management (4 marks · 4 hrs)

Staff duty rotation and the importance of continued medical education/training for laboratory personnel.

Ch. 6 Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) (5 marks · 5 hrs)

  • Introduction to Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
  • Essential components of an SOP
  • Significance of SOPs in clinical practice

Ch. 7 Clinical Laboratory Inventory Management (9 marks · 10 hrs)

  • Clinical laboratory records; Requisition slips / Test Request Form (TRF)
  • Lab report formats for different sections
  • Record keeping and its importance
  • Maintaining stock inventory and expiry charts
  • Instrument maintenance sheets (refrigerator, incubator)
  • Daily cleaning record sheets
  • Logistics management
  • Importance of computers and Laboratory Information Systems (LIS) in a modern lab

Ch. 8 Lab Hazards and Lab Safety (9 marks · 10 hrs)

Importance and uses of safety/protection in clinical laboratory practice, including 0.5–1% hypochlorite preparation and use of spill kits.

  • Management of needle-stick injury and spillage accidents, with first aid
  • Common laboratory accidents and their first aid
  • Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) and hazardous chart symbols

Common lab hazards

  • Unsafe premises
  • Naked flames/fire
  • Microbial/biological hazards
  • Chemical hazards and explosions
  • Sharps and glassware
  • Unreliable water supply
  • Infestation by ants, rodents

Safety measures include personal protective equipment (PPE), staff health and safety, and equipment/procedural safety.

Ch. 9 Total Quality Management (TQM) & Quality Assurance (15 marks · 15 hrs)

The single highest-marks unit in this subject — nearly a fifth of the total.

TQM framework: Quality Assessment, Quality Assurance, Quality Laboratory Procedures, Quality Control (internal and external).

Quality control charts — the Levey-Jennings (L-J) chart.

Terminology used in Quality Assurance

  • Accuracy vs precision
  • Sensitivity vs specificity
  • Standard and control (positive, negative, high/normal/low sera)
  • Standard deviation (SD) and coefficient of variation (CV)
  • Mean

Also know: sources of error — pre-analytical, analytical, post-analytical — and methods of improving laboratory reports.

Ch. 10 Bio-safety and Bio-security (9 marks · 10 hrs)

  • Introduction to bio-safety and bio-security
  • Risk group classification of microorganisms — risk group 1 to risk group 4
  • Concept of Biosafety Levels (BSL 1 to BSL 4)
  • Laboratory waste management
    • Segregation of waste
    • Colour-coded bins — red, blue, yellow, green, black, white, sharps bins
    • Treatment and disposal of waste
  • Concept of 3R (reduce, reuse, recycle)

Ch. 11 Medical Ethics (5 marks · 6 hrs)

Medical ethics and its principles; confidentiality; ethical consent.

Brief introduction to accrediting bodies:

  • National Public Health Laboratory (NPHL), Teku, Nepal
  • International Standardization Organization (ISO — in lab)
  • National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories (NABL)
  • The College of American Pathologists (CAP) & CLIA
  • Nepal Health Professional Council (NHPC)

Qn Practice Questions — Clinical Laboratory Management

Group A · 1 mark each
  1. Define Standard Operating Procedure (SOP).
  2. What does BSL stand for?
  3. Define coefficient of variation.
  4. Name one colour-coded waste bin and what it's for.
  5. Define accuracy in a QC context.
Group B · 3–5 marks
  1. Differentiate between internal and external quality control.
  2. Explain the management of a needle-stick injury in the lab.
  3. Describe the components of laboratory work flow, from specimen collection to report.
Group C · 8–10 marks
  1. Describe the Total Quality Management framework and the terminologies used in Quality Assurance (accuracy, precision, sensitivity, specificity, SD, CV).
  2. Discuss laboratory hazards and the safety measures used to prevent them, including PPE and MSDS.
  3. Explain the risk group classification of microorganisms and the corresponding Biosafety Levels (BSL 1–4).
