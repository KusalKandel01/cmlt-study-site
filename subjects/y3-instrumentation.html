✓ What To Do / What To Skip

DO

  • Automation (30 marks) and Instrumentation (19 marks) together are over 60% of the subject — start there.
  • Learn the 4 immunoassay platforms (ELISA/RIA/CLIA/ECLIA) as a comparison table — a very likely Group B/C question.
  • For every named instrument, memorize principle + one key application — that's usually the exact marks split in Group B.

DON'T

  • Don't try to memorize deep technical specs of every hi-tech instrument — focus on principle and application, which is what's actually examined.

Ch. 1 Instrumentation (19 marks · 15 hrs)

The highest-marks unit in this subject — nearly a quarter of the total. For each instrument, know: introduction, principle, types, importance, operation, applications, advantages, disadvantages, and maintenance.

  • Microscopes
  • Centrifuge
  • Micropipettes
  • Photometric systems — colorimeter, spectrophotometer, flame photometer
  • pH meter
  • Ion Selective Electrode (ISE)
  • Analytical balance
  • Bio-safety cabinets
  • Vertical laminar flow
  • Autoclave
  • Fumigator
  • Vortex mixer
  • Automated cell counter

Ch. 2 Hi-Tech Instruments and Technology (12 marks · 10 hrs)

Introduction, principle, and applications of:

  • Chromatography
  • Electrophoresis
  • Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)
  • Flow cytometry
  • High Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC)
  • Fluorescent and electron microscopy

Ch. 3 Immunochemical Techniques (19 marks · 15 hrs)

Introduction, principle, and applications of the major immunoassay platforms:

TechniqueKey idea
ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay)Antibody/antigen detection via enzyme-linked color change
RIA (Radioimmunoassay)Uses a radioactive label to quantify antigen/antibody
CLIA (Chemiluminescence Immunoassay)Uses a light-emitting chemical label for detection
ECLIA (Electrochemiluminescent Immunoassay)Light emission triggered electrochemically — used in many modern automated analyzers

Ch. 4 Automation (30 marks · 26 hrs)

The single biggest unit in this subject — over a third of the marks. For each automatic analyzer type, know introduction, working principle, components, applications, advantages, and disadvantages.

  • Continuous flow systems
  • Discrete analyzers (semi- and fully-automated)
  • Centrifugal analyzers
  • Dry chemistry analyzers
  • Robotic analyzers

Also know: blood cell analyzer (working principle, components, applications), the arterial blood gas analyzer and its applications, and the role of computers in laboratory automation.

Qn Practice Questions — Instrumentation and Automation

Group A · 1 mark each
  1. Define ELISA.
  2. Name one hi-tech instrument used for separating molecules by size/charge.
  3. What does HPLC stand for?
  4. Define centrifugal analyzer.
  5. Name two components of an automated blood cell analyzer.
Group B · 3–5 marks
  1. Differentiate between semi-automated and fully-automated discrete analyzers.
  2. Explain the principle and applications of flow cytometry.
  3. Describe the working principle of a spectrophotometer.
Group C · 8–10 marks
  1. Describe the principle, applications, advantages, and disadvantages of ELISA, RIA, CLIA, and ECLIA.
  2. Discuss the working principle, components, and applications of continuous flow and discrete automatic analyzers.
  3. Explain the principle and applications of PCR and electrophoresis in the clinical laboratory.
