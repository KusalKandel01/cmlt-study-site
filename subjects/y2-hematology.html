✓ What To Do / What To Skip

DO

  • Hematological Techniques (Unit 8) alone is 31 of 80 marks — know every named test's principle, procedure, and normal value cold.
  • Immunohematology & Blood Banking (15 marks) is the second-biggest unit — ABO/Rh grouping and Coombs' tests repeat constantly.
  • Learn the leukemoid reaction vs leukemia comparison — a recurring Group B question.

DON'T

  • Don't spend equal time on Platelet Disorders (1 mark) as on Hematological Techniques (31 marks) — triage ruthlessly by marks.
  • Don't skip Blood Parasites thinking it's minor — it directly overlaps with the Medical Parasitology subject, so it pays twice.

Ch. 1 Introduction to Hematology (3 marks · 6 hrs)

  • History, scope, and importance of hematology
  • Review of the circulatory system — functions of the heart, arteries, veins, and capillaries
  • Blood — definition, properties, composition, and functions of each cell type
  • Normal structure, shape, size, life span, and reference values of blood cells

Ch. 2 Haematopoiesis (6 marks · 10 hrs)

Haematopoiesis — the formation of blood cells; know the sites of blood formation.

  • Erythropoiesis (RBC formation)
    • Stages of cell development
    • Regulation of erythropoiesis
    • Fate of the RBC
  • Leucopoiesis (WBC formation)
    • Myeloid series — stages of development
    • Lymphocytic series — stages of development
    • Monocytic series — stages of development
    • Regulation of leucopoiesis
  • Thrombopoiesis (platelet formation)
    • Stages of cell development
    • Regulation of platelet formation

Ch. 3 Hemoglobin (5 marks · 9 hrs)

Hemoglobin — definition, structure, function, and normal range. Know the list of normal and abnormal hemoglobins.

ConditionKey facts
HaemoglobinopathiesDefinition and examples — inherited disorders of hemoglobin structure or synthesis
Sickle cell anemiaDefinition, cause, and laboratory diagnosis
ThalassemiaDefinition, cause, types, and laboratory diagnosis

Ch. 4 Red Cell Disorders (8 marks · 13 hrs)

Introduction to qualitative, quantitative, and morphological abnormalities of red cells. Anaemia — definition and classification.

Definition, causes, symptoms, lab diagnosis & treatment of:

  • Iron deficiency anemia
  • Megaloblastic anemia
  • Hemolytic anemia

Polycythemia — definition, causes, symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment (the opposite pattern — excess red cell mass).

Ch. 5 White Blood Cell Disorders (5 marks · 8 hrs)

Introduction to morphological and functional disorders of white blood cells (qualitative and quantitative).

TermDefinition
Leukemoid reactionA benign, reactive over-production of WBCs — definition, causes, lab diagnosis
LeukemiaA malignant WBC disorder — definition, causes, classification, lab diagnosis

A very common Group B question: differentiate between leukemoid reaction and leukemia.

Ch. 6 Platelet Disorders (1 marks · 2 hrs)

Introduction, definition, and classification of platelet disorders — a short unit, low marks, quick recall value only.

Ch. 7 Coagulation Mechanism (3 marks · 6 hrs)

Haemostasis — definition and components; coagulation factors; the process of haemostasis and blood coagulation.

Introduction to bleeding disorders — thrombocytopenia and hemophilia.

Ch. 8 Hematological Techniques (31 marks · 55 hrs)

The single highest-marks unit in this subject — nearly 40% of the total. This is the practical core of hematology.

Sample collection

  • Anticoagulants — definition, types by mechanism of action, uses, advantages/disadvantages
  • Phlebotomy technique
    • Anatomical sites of blood collection
    • Capillary, venous, and arterial collection methods
    • Precautions during sample collection
  • Blood smear preparation — thick and thin

Hemocytometry (principle, procedure, normal values, clinical significance)

  • Total RBC count
  • Total WBC count
  • Platelet count

Staining & counts

  • Romanowsky stains — Wright's, Leishman's, Giemsa (definition, composition, preparation, uses)
  • Differential Leukocyte Count (DLC) — principle, procedure, reference range, clinical significance
  • Absolute Eosinophil Count
  • Bone marrow examination — sample sites, smear prep, Giemsa staining

Also know: automated blood cell analyzer working principle, LE cell preparation, osmotic fragility test, sickling test, hemoglobin electrophoresis, and quality control in the hematology lab.

Ch. 9 Blood Parasites (3 marks · 6 hrs)

List the blood parasites relevant to Nepal, and know smear preparation, staining, and identification of the malarial parasite, microfilaria, and LD bodies (Leishman-Donovan bodies).

Ch. 10 Immunohematology and Blood Banking (15 marks · 25 hrs)

History, scope, and importance of blood banking; instruments and anticoagulants used.

  • Blood grouping systems and the various blood groups
  • Selection/rejection criteria for a blood donor
  • Blood donation — donor prep, collection, transport, processing, storage
  • Blood components — uses and storage

Theory, procedure, precaution & significance of:

  • ABO and Rh blood grouping
  • Cross-matching
  • Direct and indirect Coombs' tests

Also know: transfusion-transmissible infections and their screening, post-transfusion reactions, and haemolytic disease of the newborn (etiology, pathogenesis, clinical features, lab diagnosis, management) — a classic Group C essay topic.

Qn Practice Questions — Hematology and Blood Banking

Group A · 1 mark each
  1. Define anaemia.
  2. What is the normal function of platelets?
  3. Name the two methods used for ESR estimation.
  4. Define haemostasis.
  5. What is polycythemia?
Group B · 3–5 marks
  1. Differentiate between leukemoid reaction and leukemia.
  2. Explain the ABO blood grouping and cross-matching procedure.
  3. Differentiate between sickle cell anemia and thalassemia.
Group C · 8–10 marks
  1. Describe the causes, symptoms, laboratory diagnosis, and treatment of iron deficiency anemia, megaloblastic anemia, and hemolytic anemia.
  2. Explain haemolytic disease of the newborn — etiology, pathogenesis, clinical features, laboratory diagnosis, and management.
  3. Describe the principle, procedure, and clinical significance of hemoglobin estimation and red cell indices.
