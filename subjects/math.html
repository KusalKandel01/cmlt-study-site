1 Unit 19: Computer Skills (16 marks — the single biggest unit)

Bigger than any pure math topic in this subject. Pure memorization — no calculation required.

  • Input devices: Keyboard, mouse, scanner, OCR, OMR, digitizing tablet
  • Output devices: Monitor (CRT/LCD/LED), Printer (dot matrix, laser, inkjet, thermal)
  • Memory: Primary (Cache, RAM, ROM) vs Secondary (magnetic, optical, flash)
  • Ports: USB, VGA, DVI, PS/2
  • Internet uses: e-Health, e-Education, e-Government, Telemedicine — explicitly named
  • MS Word/Excel/PowerPoint: named features — table of contents, mail merge equivalent, formulas, charts, slide transitions
  • Security: viruses, antivirus, backups

2 Unit 16: Vital Statistics (6 marks — health-science-specific, was missing)

This is where generic "statistics" becomes specifically medical — measures you'll actually use in public health work.

CategoryNamed indicators
FertilityCrude birth rate, specific fertility rate, general fertility rate, total fertility rate
MortalityCrude death rate, specific death rate, infant mortality rate, maternal mortality rate, still birth rate

Group B model — "Define infant mortality rate and maternal mortality rate"

  1. Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) — number of deaths of infants under 1 year per 1,000 live births in a given year
  2. Maternal Mortality Rate (MMR) — number of maternal deaths due to pregnancy-related causes per 100,000 live births

4 Unit 1: Set Theory & Real Numbers (3 marks)

  • Set — a well-defined collection of distinct objects. Represented in Venn diagrams.
  • Union (∪) — combines all elements of both sets; Intersection (∩) — only common elements
  • Cardinality — the number of elements in a finite set, written n(A)
  • De Morgan's Law — (A∪B)′ = A′∩B′ and (A∩B)′ = A′∪B′ (named explicitly, expect a proof question)
  • Absolute value, open/closed intervals — foundational real-number concepts

5 Unit 2: Function & Graph (3 marks)

Function — a relation where each input maps to exactly one output. Domain (allowed inputs) and Range (possible outputs). Composite function — applying one function to the result of another. Exponential and Logarithmic functions — inverse operations of each other.

6 Unit 5 & 6: Straight Line & Coordinate Geometry (4 marks combined)

  • Distance between two points; slope of a line
  • Angle between two lines; conditions for perpendicularity and parallelism
  • Area of a triangle from coordinates; section formula
  • Pair of lines: the equation ax² + 2hxy + by² = 0 always represents a pair of straight lines passing through the origin — a named proof in the curriculum

7 Unit 7 & 8: Limits & Derivatives (6 marks combined)

Limit — the value a function approaches as the input approaches some value. Indeterminate forms: 0/0, ∞/∞, ∞−∞.

Derivative — the rate of change of a function (geometrically, the slope of the tangent line). Rules: sum, difference, product, quotient, and chain rule. Application: finding increasing/decreasing intervals and maximum/minimum points (maxima and minima) of a function.

8 Unit 10: Probability (3 marks)

Classical probability (theoretical, based on equally likely outcomes) vs Empirical probability (based on observed data). Addition law (for "either/or" events) and Multiplication law (for "and" events). Binomial probability distribution: P(r) = C(n,r)·pʳ·q⁽ⁿ⁻ʳ⁾ — used for repeated yes/no trials.

9 Units 11, 12, 17, 18: Statistics Foundations & Research (10 marks combined)

  • Statistics — the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data; has both utility and limitations
  • Primary vs Secondary data — collected firsthand vs already existing
  • Frequency table (grouped/ungrouped); representation via bar diagram, pie diagram, histogram, frequency polygon, ogive curve
  • Research methodology — steps of research, scientific method, and its application in medical science
  • Report writing — standard research report format and its purpose
