03 Where To Spend Your Time

Prioritize

  • Do Mechanics and Modern Physics first — together 30 of 80 marks, the two biggest units by far.
  • Memorize Ohm's Law (V=IR) and practice basic substitution problems — a numerical here is close to guaranteed.
  • Learn the medical-use facts explicitly (ultrasonic waves, thermal therapy, radioactivity in treatment) — these are named in the curriculum and easy Group A points.

Deprioritize

  • Don't try to derive formulas the curriculum marks "without proof" — memorize the result, skip the derivation entirely.
  • Don't neglect Electrostatics and Magnetism (9 marks each) just because they feel abstract — together they're worth as much as Modern Physics.

06 Unit 5: Electrostatics (9 marks)

Fundamentals

Charges can be created by friction, conduction, or induction. Surface charge density — charge per unit area.

Electrostatic field

  • Coulomb's Law — the force between two point charges is proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of distance between them
  • Dielectric constant (permittivity) — describes how a medium affects electric field strength
  • Electric potential — the potential energy per unit charge at a point
  • Capacitor — a device that stores electric charge; capacitance is its charge-storing ability
  • Series vs Parallel grouping of capacitors — combined capacitance differs by arrangement
  • Electron volt (eV) — a unit of energy used at atomic scale

07 Unit 6: Magnetism (9 marks)

Fundamentals

  • Magnetic moment — a measure of a magnet's strength and orientation
  • Pole strength — the strength of a magnetic pole
  • Coulomb's law for magnetism — force between two magnetic poles, analogous to electric Coulomb's law
  • Neutral point — a point where the magnetic field of a magnet is cancelled by Earth's magnetic field

Terrestrial magnetism (Earth's magnetism)

  • Dip — the angle a compass needle makes with the horizontal
  • Declination — the angle between magnetic north and true (geographic) north
  • Dia/Para/Ferromagnetic — three categories of how materials respond to magnetic fields (weakly repelled, weakly attracted, strongly attracted respectively)

08 Unit 7: Current Electricity (11 marks)

Electric current basics

Current is the rate of flow of charge. Ohm's Law: V = I × R (Voltage = Current × Resistance) — the single most-tested formula in this unit.

  • Resistance and resistivity — opposition to current flow; resistivity is a material property
  • Series vs Parallel resistance — total resistance combines differently in each arrangement
  • Galvanometer — a device that detects small currents; can be converted into an ammeter or voltmeter
  • Ohmic vs Non-Ohmic conductors — whether they obey Ohm's law consistently

Resistance & heat

  • Joule's Law of heating — heat produced is proportional to (current)² × resistance × time
  • EMF (electromotive force) vs terminal potential difference vs internal resistance — their relationship in a real battery/circuit
  • Electric power — measured in watts, kilowatts, and kilowatt-hours (the unit your electricity bill uses)

Chemical effect of current

Faraday's laws of electrolysis — the amount of substance deposited/liberated at an electrode is proportional to the quantity of electricity passed. Thermoelectric effect and the Peltier effect are named explicitly — heat/electricity conversion effects at junctions of different metals.

Alternating current (AC)

AC vs DC — AC periodically reverses direction, DC flows one way. Transformer — changes voltage levels using electromagnetic induction (Faraday's law).

09 Unit 2: Heat (9 marks)

Thermometry & Expansion

Types of thermometers; conversion between temperature scales (°F, °C, Kelvin). Linear, superficial, and cubical expansion of solids — how materials expand differently along 1, 2, or 3 dimensions when heated.

Calorimetry

  • Heat capacity vs specific heat capacity — total heat needed vs heat needed per unit mass
  • Latent heat of fusion (ice→water) and latent heat of steam (water→steam) — heat absorbed during phase change without temperature change
  • Effect of pressure on melting/boiling points

Hygrometry & Heat Transfer

Relative humidity — measured with wet and dry bulb hygrometers. 3 methods of heat transfer: conduction, convection, radiation. Medical use of heat radiation (thermal therapy) is explicitly named in the curriculum — direct clinical relevance.

10 Unit 3: Light (6 marks)

Reflection & Refraction

  • Laws of reflection — angle of incidence = angle of reflection
  • Refraction — bending of light passing between media; described by refractive index
  • Real vs apparent depth — why objects underwater appear shallower than they are
  • Lens formula and Lens Maker's formula — relate focal length to lens curvature/refractive index

Optical instruments

Structure of the human eye; common vision defects and their correction (relevant given this syllabus is shared with Ophthalmic Science students); simple and compound microscope magnification.

11 Unit 4: Waves and Sound (6 marks)

Waves

Longitudinal vs Transverse waves — particle vibration parallel vs perpendicular to wave direction. Superposition — waves combining. Reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference — the four fundamental wave behaviors.

Sound characteristics

  • Note, pitch, intensity, loudness, timbre — the five named characteristics of sound (common Group A list)
  • Beat / beat frequency — the pulsing effect from two close frequencies interfering
  • Decibel — the unit for sound intensity level
  • Threshold of hearing vs threshold of pain — the quietest audible sound vs the loudest tolerable sound
  • Ultrasonic waves and their medical uses — explicitly named; directly relevant (ultrasound imaging)
