03 Where To Spend Your Time

Prioritize

  • Prioritize Diversity of Life (19 marks) and Cell Biology (15 marks) — 34 of 80 marks in just 2 of 9 units.
  • Learn the 6 named genetic disorders as flashcards (name → cause) — fast to memorize, guaranteed Group A value.
  • Know the 5 plant groups in order (Algae → Bryophyta → Pteridophyta → Gymnosperm → Angiosperm) — a very common list question.

Deprioritize

  • Don't over-invest in Economic Botany's medicinal plant list beyond one read-through — only 4 marks for a lot of names; poor time-per-mark ratio.
  • Don't treat Genetics as optional because it feels like "extra" biology — it's 8 marks of pure memorization, some of the cheapest marks in the subject.

04 Unit 6: Genetics & Genetic Disorders (previously missing entirely)

Directly relevant to lab diagnostic work — recognizing these conditions is part of the job.

DisorderCause
Down's SyndromeExtra copy of chromosome 21 (trisomy)
Edward's SyndromeExtra copy of chromosome 18
Turner's SyndromeMissing/partial X chromosome (females)
Klinefelter's SyndromeExtra X chromosome in males (XXY)
AlbinismGene mutation affecting melanin production
HaemophiliaX-linked recessive; blood fails to clot properly

Mendel's Laws (foundation for all of the above)

  1. Law of Dominance — one allele masks the expression of the other in a heterozygote
  2. Law of Segregation — the two alleles for a trait separate during gamete formation
  3. Law of Independent Assortment — genes for different traits are inherited independently of each other

Mutation types: Gene mutation (substitution, frameshift, nonsense) vs Chromosomal mutation (aberration, ploidy).

07 Unit 3: Cell Biology (15 marks)

Cell wall, cell membrane (fluid mosaic model), organelles (mitochondria, plastids, ER, Golgi, ribosomes), nucleus, chromosomes. Cell division: Mitosis (growth/repair) vs Meiosis (gametes) — know the phase-by-phase differences, a very common Group B "differentiate between" question.

09 Unit 1 & 2: Introduction & Life Components (8 marks combined)

Botany — the scientific study of plants. Its branches connect to health science through pharmacognosy (plant-derived medicines) and ethnobotany.

Biomolecules (Unit 2)

BiomoleculeRole
CarbohydratesEnergy source and structural component
LipidsEnergy storage, membrane structure
Amino acids/ProteinsStructural and functional (enzymes)
Nucleic acids (DNA/RNA)Genetic information storage and expression; DNA is a double helix

Inorganic biomolecules (minerals, water) also have named roles and deficiency conditions — parallels the "biological importance of minerals" content in Chemistry Unit 2.7.

10 Unit 7: Environmental Biology (8 marks)

Ecosystem components

  • Abiotic factors — climatic, edaphic (soil), topographic
  • Biotic factors — autotrophs (producers) and heterotrophs (consumers)
  • Food chain vs Food web — linear vs interconnected feeding relationships
  • Ecological pyramid — represents energy/biomass/numbers at each trophic level

Pollution & hazards

Air pollution and water pollution — causes, health consequences, prevention. Greenhouse effect, ozone layer depletion, acid rain — each with named causes and control measures. Biological invasion — spread of non-native species disrupting ecosystems.

12 Unit 9: Biotechnology (6 marks)

  • Plant tissue culture — growing plant cells/tissue in controlled lab conditions to produce clones
  • Biofertilizer — biological substances that enrich soil nutrients
  • Genetic engineering — direct manipulation of DNA; insulin production is the named example (bacteria engineered to produce human insulin — a landmark biotech achievement)
  • DNA fingerprinting/profiling — identifying individuals by unique DNA patterns (forensics, paternity testing)
  • Fermentation technology — using microorganisms to produce useful products (antibiotics, alcohol, etc.)
