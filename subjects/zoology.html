1 Binomial Nomenclature

Every organism is named using two parts: Genus + species (e.g. Homo sapiens). Genus is capitalized, species is lowercase, both italicized. Devised by Carl Linnaeus — a common Group A fact.

2 Five Kingdom Classification

KingdomKey feature
MoneraSingle-celled, no nucleus (bacteria)
ProtistaSingle-celled, has nucleus (amoeba)
FungiCannot make own food, absorbs nutrients (mushroom)
PlantaeMulticellular, makes own food (plants)
AnimaliaMulticellular, cannot make own food (animals)

4 Characteristics of Phylum Chordata

This is the phylum humans belong to — a favorite Group B question.

  1. Presence of a notochord (a flexible rod along the back) at some stage of life
  2. Dorsal (back-side) hollow nerve cord
  3. Paired gill slits at some stage of life
  4. A post-anal tail at some stage of life

6 Cell Division Basics

  • Mitosis — one cell divides into two identical daughter cells; used for growth and repair
  • Meiosis — one cell divides into four daughter cells with half the chromosome number; used for producing gametes (sperm/egg)

7 Group B Model — "Write the characteristics of Kingdom Animalia"

  1. Multicellular organisms
  2. Cannot prepare their own food (heterotrophic)
  3. Cells lack a cell wall
  4. Most can move from place to place
  5. Reproduce sexually in most cases
