1 Unit 4: Medically Important Parasites (25 marks — highest single unit)

This is the most directly job-relevant content in your entire first year — identifying these organisms is literally what a lab technician does. The curriculum wants, for each: systematic position, habitat, life cycle, transmission, pathogenic effect, prevention.

Medically important arthropods

  • Sarcoptes scabiei (mange mite) — causes scabies
  • Anopheles, Culex, Aedes (mosquitoes) — vectors for malaria, filariasis, dengue respectively
  • Periplaneta americana (cockroach) — mechanical disease carrier

Integrated Vector Management (IVM) — a combined approach (environmental, chemical, biological control) to controlling disease-carrying vectors like mosquitoes, named explicitly in the curriculum.

Group B model — "Define host and parasite. Give two examples."

  1. Host — an organism that harbors and provides nutrition to a parasite (e.g., humans are the host for Ascaris lumbricoides)
  2. Parasite — an organism that lives on or in a host and derives nutrition at the host's expense (e.g., Plasmodium vivax in the human bloodstream)

2 Unit 5: Life Process of Mammals (27 marks — the single biggest unit)

Six mammalian systems. This overlaps with Anatomy & Physiology but is tested separately here — don't skip it assuming A&P covers it.

SystemKey exam-relevant fact
DigestiveAlimentary canal: mouth → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine. Enzymes digest carbohydrates, proteins, lipids separately.
RespiratoryGas exchange in lungs/tissue; internal respiration includes the Bohr effect and chloride shift (named mechanisms — high Group A yield)
CirculatoryDouble circulation: systemic + pulmonary. Heartbeat origin, conduction, and regulation; blood grouping
ExcretoryNephron structure; urine formation = glomerular filtration → selective reabsorption → tubular secretion
ReproductiveMale/female organ structure; menstruation cycle
NervousCentral/peripheral/autonomous divisions; nerve impulse transmission

Group B model — "Explain the mechanism of urine formation"

  1. Glomerular filtration — blood is filtered under pressure in the glomerulus, forming filtrate
  2. Selective reabsorption — useful substances (glucose, water, ions) are reabsorbed back into blood in the tubules
  3. Tubular secretion — waste substances are actively secreted into the tubule from surrounding blood

4 Unit 1 & 3: Introduction & Diversity (4 marks combined)

Zoology branches relevant to health science: parasitology, microbiology, pathology, toxicology, entomology, helminthology — these branch names directly map to your future lab specializations.

Binomial nomenclature — Carolus Linnaeus's two-part naming system (Genus + species). Five kingdom system: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.

5 Unit 6: Evolution (5 marks)

  • Lamarckism — theory of inheritance of acquired characteristics (now largely rejected, but historically important)
  • Darwinism — natural selection: organisms with favorable traits survive and reproduce more
  • Neo-Darwinism — modern synthetic theory, combining Darwinism with genetics
  • Evidence types: morphological/anatomical, paleontological (fossil), biochemical
  • Human evolution — traced from ancestor Dryopithecus

6 Unit 7: Wildlife Conservation (4 marks)

Categories of wildlife and conservation status; causes of extinction (habitat loss, poaching, pollution); Nepal's protected areas (national parks, wildlife reserves, conservation areas) as a named topic.

7 Unit 8: Application of Biology (4 marks)

  • Antibiotics, immunity, vaccines — definitions and role in human health
  • Organ transplantation — tissue/organ transplant examples
  • Amniocentesis — prenatal diagnostic procedure sampling amniotic fluid
  • Test-tube baby — in-vitro fertilization (IVF) process, briefly

8 Unit 9: Poisonous & Nonpoisonous Snakes (2 marks — high rural clinical relevance)

  • General characteristics of snakes; distinguishing features of poisonous vs non-poisonous species
  • Common poisonous snakes found in Nepal and their geographic distribution
  • How to identify a poisonous vs non-poisonous snakebite
  • Venom types and their effects on the body
  • First-aid treatment of snakebite — practically vital for rural health posts
