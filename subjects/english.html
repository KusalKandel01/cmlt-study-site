1 Parts of Speech

PartFunctionExample
NounNames a person, place, or thingdoctor, hospital

AdjectiveDescribes a nounhealthy, small
AdverbDescribes a verb/adjectivequickly, very
PrepositionShows relation (place/time)in, on, at
ConjunctionJoins words/clausesand, but, because

2 Tenses — The Core Pattern

The 3 main tenses × 4 forms each = 12 total. Focus on recognizing the pattern:

TenseSimpleContinuous
PresentI writeI am writing
PastI wroteI was writing
FutureI will writeI will be writing

3 Subject-Verb Agreement

  • Singular subject → singular verb: "She works."
  • Plural subject → plural verb: "They work."
  • Common exam trap: collective nouns like "team", "family" usually take a singular verb: "The team is ready."

4 Articles

a/an — used before a singular, non-specific noun (a before consonant sounds, an before vowel sounds). the — used before a specific noun already known to the reader.

5 Formal Letter Structure (Group C staple)

  1. Sender's address (top right or left)
  2. Date
  3. Receiver's address
  4. Subject line
  5. Salutation ("Dear Sir/Madam,")
  6. Body (introduction → main point → conclusion)
  7. Closing ("Yours faithfully,") + signature

6 Essay Structure

  1. Introduction — introduce the topic in 2-3 sentences
  2. Body paragraphs — one clear point per paragraph, with an example
  3. Conclusion — summarize and give a final thought

7 Comprehension Strategy

Read the questions before the passage — you'll know what to look for. Answers to factual questions are almost always found in the same order the questions are asked, in the same order the information appears in the passage.
