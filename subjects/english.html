3 Grammar Points — All 11 Units Explained

Each named grammar point from the unit table, actually explained.

Grammar pointExplanation
Review of TensesPresent/Past/Future × Simple/Continuous/Perfect/Perfect Continuous — 12 combinations total; match tense to when the action happens relative to now
Direct & Indirect SpeechDirect: "I am tired," she said. Indirect: She said (that) she was tired. Tense shifts back one step; pronouns and time words change
Future Formswill/shall (prediction), going to (plan), present continuous (arrangement) — each implies different certainty
PassivesActive: "The nurse gave the injection." Passive: "The injection was given (by the nurse)." Used when the doer is unknown/unimportant — common in clinical writing
Making Wishes"I wish I had studied" (past regret), "I wish I were taller" (present unreal) — uses past/past perfect forms for hypotheticals
Root Words & Prefixese.g., "bio-" (life) + "-logy" (study) = biology; recognizing roots helps decode unfamiliar medical vocabulary
British/American PronunciationSpelling and pronunciation differences (colour/color, schedule pronunciation)
Degrees of CertaintyModal verbs expressing confidence: must (certain) → could/might (possible) → can't (impossible)
Giving Adviceshould (mild recommendation), would (conditional), could (a suggested option) — used constantly when writing patient advice
Dictionary/Alphabetical OrderLocating and ordering words — a basic reference skill unit
Homophones & PrepositionsPrincipal/Principle-type word pairs; prepositions of time (at/on/in) and place (at/on/in) — commonly confused pairs

4 Vocabulary Themes — All 11 Units

5 Writing Formats — Group C Staples

Formal Letter

  1. Sender's address + Date
  2. Receiver's address
  3. Subject line
  4. Salutation ("Dear Sir/Madam,")
  5. Body: introduction → main point → conclusion
  6. Closing ("Yours faithfully,") + signature

Academic Essay (5-paragraph, per Unit 4's explicit project work)

  1. Introduction
  2. Body paragraph 1
  3. Body paragraph 2
  4. Body paragraph 3
  5. Conclusion using linking phrases
