1 Basic Definitions (Group A)

  • Atom — the smallest particle of an element that can take part in a chemical reaction
  • Molecule — the smallest particle of a substance formed by two or more atoms bonded together
  • Element — a pure substance made of only one type of atom
  • Compound — a substance formed by chemically combining two or more elements in a fixed ratio
  • Mixture — two or more substances combined physically (not chemically), in any ratio

2 Atomic Structure

ParticleChargeLocation
ProtonPositive (+)Nucleus
NeutronNeutral (no charge)Nucleus
ElectronNegative (−)Orbits around the nucleus

Atomic number = number of protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons.

3 Mole Concept

Mole — the amount of substance containing as many particles as there are atoms in 12g of carbon-12. One mole of any substance contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number) — a very commonly asked fill-in value.

4 Acids, Bases & Salts

TermDefinitionExample
AcidA substance that releases H⁺ ions in water; pH below 7HCl, H₂SO₄
BaseA substance that releases OH⁻ ions in water; pH above 7NaOH, KOH
SaltFormed when an acid reacts with a base (neutralization)NaCl

pH scale runs 0–14. Below 7 = acidic, 7 = neutral, above 7 = basic/alkaline. Normal blood pH is 7.35–7.45 (slightly alkaline) — a genuinely high-yield fact for your future lab work too.

5 Chemical Bonding

  • Ionic bond — formed by the transfer of electrons from one atom to another (e.g. NaCl: Na gives an electron to Cl)
  • Covalent bond — formed by the sharing of electrons between atoms (e.g. H₂O)

6 Periodic Table Basics

Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number. Horizontal rows are called periods; vertical columns are called groups. Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties because they have the same number of electrons in their outermost shell.

7 Organic Chemistry Basics

Hydrocarbons are compounds made only of carbon and hydrogen, classified by the type of bond between carbon atoms:

TypeBondGeneral formulaExample
AlkaneSingle bond onlyCₙH₂ₙ₊₂Methane (CH₄)
AlkeneOne double bondCₙH₂ₙEthene (C₂H₄)
AlkyneOne triple bondCₙH₂ₙ₋₂Ethyne (C₂H₂)

8 Group B Model — "Differentiate between acids and bases"

This "differentiate between X and Y" question format is extremely common — always answer as a short comparison list, not prose.

  1. Acids taste sour; bases taste bitter
  2. Acids turn blue litmus red; bases turn red litmus blue
  3. Acids have pH below 7; bases have pH above 7
  4. Acids release H⁺ ions in water; bases release OH⁻ ions
